# The offer ideas this skill works from

These are ideas from the book *$100M Offers* by Alex Hormozi, summed up in our own words. Nothing
here is quoted from the book. This skill is independent: it is not made, reviewed or endorsed by
Alex Hormozi or Acquisition.com. The book explains all of this far better and is worth buying.

## 1. Value is four things, two up and two down

A buyer weighs an offer on four things. Two make it worth more: how much they want the result,
and how sure they are that they will get it. Two make it worth less: how long until the result
arrives, and how much effort, risk or giving-up it takes from them. Most offers only talk about
the first. The cheapest gains are usually in the other three: show proof, shorten the wait to a
first result, and take work off the buyer.

## 2. Start from the buyer's problems, in order

List every problem the buyer meets on the way to the result: before they start, while they work,
when it gets hard, after the first win. Each problem becomes something the offer solves. The
problems that come next for the buyer are the best extras, because they are already worried about
them.

## 3. Bundle it so it can't be compared on price alone

When an offer is the same as everyone else's, buyers compare prices and the cheapest wins. When it
is a different bundle of solutions, with its own name, buyers compare it to the result instead.
Keep what solves real problems, cut what is only filler, and name the package after the result for
the buyer it is for.

## 4. Take the risk off the buyer

A buyer who fears losing money or looking foolish waits. A guarantee, a trial, paying after the
first result, or a promise to keep working until a result arrives moves that risk to the seller.
It only works if the seller will really honour it, so it is the owner's decision, never the
writer's. A guarantee tied to the buyer doing their part is often safer and more believable than
an unconditional one.

## 5. Give an honest reason to act now

Real limits (only so many clients a month can be served well) and real deadlines (a cohort that
starts on a date, a price that will rise) help a buyer decide. Invented ones destroy trust and can
be illegal. This skill only uses limits and deadlines the owner has approved as true.

## 6. Price on value, not on cost or on the market

When the bundle is worth far more than it costs, the price can sit above the market and still feel
like a good deal, and a higher price funds better delivery. The price is set against the value of
the result to the buyer, then checked against what competitors publish (offer-price) and against
what each sale leaves after its own costs.

## How the skill uses them

- The scorecard's seven questions (outcome, proof, speed, ease, risk, stack, now) come from ideas 1,
  2, 4 and 5, phrased as questions we wrote.
- The rebuild walks ideas 2, 3, 4 and 5 in order, with the owner, using only facts the owner
  approves.
- The price comes from offer-price's approved moves (idea 6).
- The copy check enforces idea 5 and the risk rule in idea 4 in code: no guarantee, limit, deadline,
  number or extra goes into the copy unless the owner approved it.
